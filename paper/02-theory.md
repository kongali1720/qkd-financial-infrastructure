# Chapter 2 - Theoretical Foundations

## 2.1 Quantum Mechanics

QKD uses properties of quantum states and measurement to support the establishment of shared secret keys.

## 2.2 No-Cloning Theorem

An arbitrary unknown quantum state cannot be perfectly cloned.

## 2.3 BB84 Protocol

BB84 uses randomly selected quantum states and measurement bases, followed by authenticated classical reconciliation.

## 2.4 Quantum Bit Error Rate

QBER is estimated to determine whether security conditions permit key extraction.

## 2.5 Classical Post-Processing

Important stages include:

- Basis reconciliation
- Parameter estimation
- Information reconciliation
- Verification
- Privacy amplification
- Authentication

## 2.6 Post-Quantum Cryptography

Relevant NIST standards include:

- FIPS 203: ML-KEM
- FIPS 204: ML-DSA
- FIPS 205: SLH-DSA

PQC algorithms run on conventional computing infrastructure and do not require quantum communication hardware.
